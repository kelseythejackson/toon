Realistic motion often looks flat on screen. Exaggeration pushes each pose further from rest (bigger eyes, a deeper crouch, a higher jump) so the idea is clear at a glance. It's a dial, not a switch: a game might want 1.8 and a banking app 0.5.

Every preset multiplies its squash, height and swing by `--toon-exaggeration`, so one value tunes a whole product. For fixed levels, use the `.toon-subtle` (0.5) and `.toon-exaggerate` (1.8) modifiers instead.

## Exaggeration: tuning tips

- **Exaggerate the important beat, not every frame.** In a [take](../../glossary/#take), the surprise pose gets the big push and everything else stays readable.
- **Stay consistent.** Pick one exaggeration level per product or scene; mixing levels feels accidental.
- **Clamp the results.** High factors can turn a scale negative or push a shape off the canvas. The snippet keeps the height scale at 0.3 or more.
- **Exaggerate in time, too.** `ease.overshoot()` and `ease.elastic()` fly past the target before settling, which reads as extra energy without bigger poses.

The demo also shows [anticipation](../anticipation/): the squint before the take makes the jump land harder.

For every option, see the [Canvas `exaggerate` function](../../api/canvas/#physics) and the [CSS custom properties](../../api/css/#custom-properties).
