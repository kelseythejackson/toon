Timing is how long an action takes; [spacing](../../glossary/#spacing) is how the motion is spread across that time. Together they tell the viewer how heavy something is: a bowling ball drops hard and barely bounces, a rubber ball rebounds fast, and a balloon drifts. In the demo, Brick plays the bowling ball, Dot the rubber ball and Zip the balloon.

## Timing: tuning tips

- **Make heavy things slow to start and quick to stop.** A bowling ball takes a while to get going, lands abruptly, deforms little and barely rebounds.
- **Let light things react at once.** A balloon or a leaf floats, flutters and overshoots, so give it low gravity and plenty of drag.
- **Keep interface timing short.** Use 150–250 ms for small feedback and 300–500 ms for larger moves across the screen. Anything longer starts to feel slow.
- **Check timing in slow motion.** Set `Toon.ticker.timeScale` to 0.25 to slow every animation on the clock, like the demo's *Time scale* slider. In CSS, a `--toon-tempo` of 4 on `:root` does the same.

The demo also shows [squash and stretch](../squash-and-stretch/): each object deforms as much as its material allows.

For every option, see the [Canvas timing functions](../../api/canvas/#timing-and-sequencing) and the [CSS custom properties](../../api/css/#custom-properties).
