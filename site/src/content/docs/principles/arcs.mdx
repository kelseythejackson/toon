Almost nothing in nature moves in a straight line. A thrown ball traces a curve (a *parabola*), a turning head sweeps an arc, and a waving hand follows a curve. Straight-line motion reads as robotic.

## Arcs: tuning tips

- **Keep the timing linear.** Easing the progress along `arc()` makes the throw float or slam. A `tween` with `ease.linear` does the same job as the steady clock in the snippet.
- **Curve through more points with `path()`.** It draws a smooth curve through every point you give it (a [Catmull-Rom curve](../../glossary/#catmull-rom-curve)), and `tangent()` works on it too.
- **Check your arcs with ghosts.** Plot past positions, as the demos do. Kinks and flat spots show up immediately.
- **Curve interface paths too.** A slight curve on a card flying to a cart, or a dialog swooping in, feels more natural than a straight slide.

The arcs demo also shows [squash and stretch](../squash-and-stretch/): Dot stretches along the direction of travel. The [throwing recipe](../../recipes/#throwing-something-along-a-path) adds that to an arc.
