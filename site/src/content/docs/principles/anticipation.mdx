Before jumping, you crouch. Before throwing, you pull your arm back. Anticipation is a small move in the **opposite direction** of the main action. It prepares the viewer's eye, so the action reads clearly even when it's fast.

`.toon-press` dips while held and springs past its size on release. `.toon-anticipate` crouches before it rises, and `.toon-launch` pulls back before it shoots forward. For a transition of your own, the `--toon-ease-anticipate` easing dips backwards first.

## Anticipation in Canvas

Write the action as beats with `sequence`, and make the first beat the wind-up. Each beat is a `tween` of the ball's height and vertical scale. For a single tween, `ease.anticipate(s)` puts the dip inside the easing curve itself. `s` sets how far it dips: 1.7 by default, 0 for none.

## Anticipation: tuning tips

- **Size the wind-up to the action.** A small action needs only a small wind-up. For a big leap, the crouch is the longest beat before it: 280 ms against a 90 ms take-off in the Canvas snippet.
- **Give fast actions more anticipation.** If the main move lasts only a few frames, the wind-up is what makes it readable.
- **Move against the action.** A wind-up reads only when it goes the other way: down before up, back before forward.
- **Keep it subtle in interfaces.** The presets are sized to read in a demo: `.toon-press` dips about 9%. On a business screen, add `.toon-subtle` to halve every wind-up, to about 4%.

The Dot demo also shows [squash and stretch](../squash-and-stretch/) (Dot stretches on take-off and squashes on landing) and [slow in and slow out](../slow-in-slow-out/) (the rise slows into the top of the jump).
