Staging means directing attention, so the viewer looks at one thing at a time. Use contrast in color, light, motion and size to lead the eye, and give the important thing a clear silhouette and a moment to itself. In the demo, Loop performs under the spotlight while a crowd of gray Bricks stands still and watches.

When one child is featured, hovered or focused, its siblings fade to 40% and shrink to 96% in 300 ms. Only `opacity` and `scale` change, so the browser can run it on the GPU; for a pointed light instead, lay a `.toon-spotlight` overlay over the group.

## Staging in Canvas

Draw the scene, then call `stage.spotlight()` to darken everything outside a soft circle. The light's position runs through a `Spring`, so it glides to each new performer instead of jumping. The performer also stands out by color, size and motion.

## Staging: tuning tips

- **Animate one thing at a time.** If everything moves, nothing stands out. Keep the crowd still or on slow idle motion.
- **Use several kinds of contrast together.** Color, brightness, motion and size reinforce each other. The Canvas snippet uses all four.
- **Move focus smoothly.** A sudden jump disorients the viewer. A short eased glide (300 ms in CSS, a soft spring in Canvas) keeps their place.
- **Fade, don't blur.** Animating `filter: blur()` on the background repaints every sibling on every frame. Fading and shrinking reads the same and stays smooth.

In the demo, the crowd drifts on slow noise, a [secondary action](../secondary-action/) that keeps the scene alive without pulling focus.
