When a body stops, its loose parts (a tail, hair, ears, a cape) keep going, swing past, then settle back. That's **follow-through**. **Overlapping action** is the companion idea: parts move at different rates and start at different times, so nothing moves in lock-step.

Each item starts `--toon-overlap` (70 ms by default) after the one before it. It turns from its top edge, so its bottom swings past the stop and wobbles back; use `.toon-settle` for the same entrance on one element.

## Follow-through in Canvas

This ball runs as-is. The body follows its target on a slightly under-damped `Spring`, so it overshoots a little. Each antenna is a `Chain` ([simple rope physics](../../glossary/#rope-physics-verlet)) with its own length and damping, so the two drag, swing past and settle out of step.

## Follow-through: tuning tips

- **Let lighter parts lag more.** Hair and fabric overlap more than limbs. Give each chain its own `damping`, closer to 1 for the floppiest parts.
- **Keep the settle short.** Two or three shrinking swings are plenty. A long wobble looks like jelly.
- **Keep stagger delays small.** 40–90 ms between items reads as one fluid motion. Longer gaps read as separate events.
- **Stagger scripted sequences too.** In Canvas, `Toon.stagger(items, fn, { each: 80 })` starts each item 80 ms after the last, like `--toon-overlap` does in CSS.

For every option, see the [Canvas `Spring` and `Chain`](../../api/canvas/#physics) and the [CSS `.toon-follow` preset](../../api/css/#effects).
