An object flattens when it hits something and stretches when it moves fast. The trick that keeps it believable is **constant volume**: as it gets wider it gets shorter. Every squash and stretch in toon keeps its width scale times its height scale at 1.

`.toon-bounce` moves the ball the way gravity would: slow at the top, fast at the floor. It squashes at contact, stretches at top speed and goes round near the top. It scales from the bottom edge, so the squash stays on the ground. `--toon-height` sets how high it bounces (140px by default).

## Squash & stretch in Canvas

This ball runs as-is. `impact()` flattens it while it's on the floor. `squash()` stretches it along its velocity, so a ball thrown sideways stretches sideways. `withSquash()` draws it, with a [pivot](../../glossary/#pivot) that keeps its base on the floor.

## Squash & stretch: tuning tips

- **Hold the contact for a few frames.** Keeping the ball on the ground for about 70–90 ms while squashed reads much better than squashing as it leaves.
- **Match squash to the material.** `--toon-squash` is `.22` by default, about a tennis ball. Use `.toon-heavy` for a bowling ball (.06) and `.toon-light` for rubber (.38). Rigid objects shouldn't deform at all.
- **Keep interfaces subtle.** A button that squashes like rubber looks broken. Around .1 reads as pressable; save .38 and more for characters.
- **Avoid deforming at rest.** Squash and stretch belong to impacts and high speed. An object that sits still should keep its shape.

The Dot demo also shows [timing](../timing/) (gravity sets the beat), [slow in and slow out](../slow-in-slow-out/) (the trail dots bunch up at the top of each bounce) and [arcs](../arcs/) (the trail is a smooth curve).
