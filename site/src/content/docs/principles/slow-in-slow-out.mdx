Real objects speed up out of a pose (slow out) and slow down into the next one (slow in). Together they're called *easing*. Animators check it with a **spacing chart**, one mark per frame: marks bunched together mean slow, marks spread apart mean fast. Even spacing looks mechanical.

## Slow in & slow out: tuning tips

- **Mind the names.** "Slow in" is an *ease-out* curve, because it slows into the end pose. Use `--toon-ease-out` for things arriving and `--toon-ease-in` for things leaving.
- **Ease in and out between two rest poses.** A panel that slides from one place to another needs both ends soft, so use `--toon-ease-in-out`. To match a CSS curve exactly in Canvas, pass its four numbers to `ease.cubicBezier()`.
- **Use quadratics for gravity.** Anything thrown or falling should use quadratic easing: `--toon-ease-rise` and `--toon-ease-fall` in CSS, and `outQuad` and `inQuad` in Canvas.
- **Use linear only on purpose**, for machines, scrolling backgrounds, or one axis of an [arc](../arcs/).

For every option, see the [Canvas easing functions](../../api/canvas/#easing) and the [CSS easing variables](../../api/css/#custom-properties).
