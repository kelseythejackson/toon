Appeal is charisma, and it isn't the same as cuteness: a villain can be appealing too. It comes from clear, simple shapes, pleasing proportions and signs of life. A character that breathes, blinks and reacts feels present even when it's doing nothing. In the demo, Dot does all three.

## Appeal in CSS

To give a character life at rest, add `.toon-pop-in` to a wrapper, `.toon-breathe` to the body and `.toon-blink` to the eyes. This snippet runs as-is:

## Appeal in Canvas

`breathe()` returns a small scale for `withSquash()`, so the body swells and settles. `blink()` returns how far the lids are closed, from 0 to 1, with uneven gaps between blinks. Two `Spring` objects move the eyes toward the pointer and let them settle softly.

## Appeal: tuning tips

- **Simplify the silhouette.** If you can't recognize the shape filled in black, simplify it.
- **Vary proportions.** Big and small shapes together are more interesting than all-medium ones: big eyes on a round body read at a glance.
- **Keep idle motion subtle.** A scale change of 2–4% is enough. More makes the character look anxious.
- **Avoid a metronome.** `blink()` varies the gap between blinks, and a breath of about 3 seconds keeps the two rhythms from lining up.

The Dot demo also shows [secondary action](../secondary-action/) (blinking while breathing) and [follow-through](../follow-through/) (the eyes settle on springs).
