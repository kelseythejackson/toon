The primary action is what a character is doing, such as walking. Secondary actions support it: arms swinging against the step, eyes glancing around, an occasional blink. They make the main action richer, but they never compete with it.

`.toon-sway` tilts 4 degrees each way from the bottom edge, `.toon-bob` floats up 5px and back, and `.toon-blink` squashes to a line about once every 4 seconds. Each runs on a multiple of `--toon-duration`, so they drift in and out of step instead of pulsing together.

## Secondary action: tuning tips

- **Keep amplitudes small.** If people notice the secondary action first, it's too big. The arms here swing about 80 degrees; the eyes move 6px at most.
- **Drive it from the primary motion.** Arms that read the walk's phase stay in step for free, while an independent loop can feel disconnected.
- **Give idle motion its own clock.** Glances and blinks that follow the walk look mechanical. A slow `noise()` and an irregular blink feel like a mind at work.
- **Turn it off and compare.** Untick *Secondary action* in the demos: the walk still works, but the character looks lifeless.
